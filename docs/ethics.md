# Ethics and intended use

Lightman measures people. That is useful and it can do harm. These rules come with it.

## Intended use

* Reviewing your own sessions, interviews, presentations or practice conversations.
* Supporting a practitioner's reflection on a session with a client who agreed to it.
* Research with informed consent and ethics approval where your institution requires it.

## Not for

* **Hiring, admissions, promotion or any decision about a person's opportunities.**
* **Interrogation, police or security screening, border control, or fraud detection.**
* **Recording or analysing anyone without their informed consent**, including covert use in
  calls or meetings.
* **Diagnosis.** Lightman does not detect depression, anxiety, autism, attention disorders,
  pain or any other condition, and must not be presented as doing so.
* **Deciding whether someone is lying.** The deception-research cues are weak (human accuracy
  with all of them is about 54%) and are hidden by default for that reason.
* Monitoring employees, students or family members.

## Consent

Consent is part of the workflow, not a checkbox at install time. Before each session the
practitioner records who agreed, how, and to what. Analysis does not start without it, and
voice analysis, transcripts and recordings only run when they are in the agreed scope.

Informed consent means the person understood:

* what is measured (face, voice, posture) and what is not (thoughts, feelings, truthfulness);
* where the data is stored and for how long;
* that they can stop at any time and ask to see or delete what was recorded.

People in a dependent relationship with you (clients, students, employees) may find it hard to
say no. Make refusing easy and consequence-free.

## Data

* Use client codes, not names. Labels stay on your machine.
* Keep the computer encrypted (FileVault, BitLocker, LUKS) and locked.
* Export a client's data when they ask for it; erase it when they ask for that.
* Reports contain face images. Share them only with the person shown, or with their consent.

## Interpretation

* Every measure is relative to the same person's calibration. Do not compare numbers between
  people.
* Expressed affect describes the face and voice, not the feeling. People smile through distress.
* Culture, neurodivergence, medication, tiredness and the camera itself change how faces and
  voices move. A change is a reason to ask, never a conclusion.

## If something goes wrong

If you learn that Lightman is being used against these rules, or that its output harmed
someone, open an issue or contact the maintainer privately through GitHub.
